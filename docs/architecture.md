# Architecture

How RustyZoo is built, and the choices contributors share. What is not written yet is marked **Planned**. The plan and its progress are in the [milestones](https://github.com/RustyZoo/RustyZoo/milestones).

## Layout

One Cargo package, no workspace until a second consumer exists.

- The core library (`src/lib.rs`) has no platform code. It reads the game data and draws into memory.
- The program (`src/main.rs`) holds the commands and the window. SDL3 is used only here.

Modules of the core:

| Module | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `vfs` | Read-only file system over the loose files and archives of the install folder |
| `config` | Reader of the game's INI-like files (`.ai`, `.cfg`, `.lyt`, ...) |
| `pe` | Bounded reader for the resources of the game's resource DLLs |
| `strings` | The game's texts, merged from the `lang*.dll` libraries |
| `text` | Liberation Sans Regular and Bold (embedded) with the metrics of Arial: line height, baseline, whole-pixel advances; wrapping, alignment, clipping, shadow and drawing into a canvas; templates, numbers, money and dates (`text::format`) |
| `sprite` | Sprite files and palettes: decoding, drawing, tinting a palette, PNG output |
| `userdir` | The per-user folder: where it is, the settings written there, read over the game's `zoo.ini` |
| `startup` | The start-up picture (a bitmap resource of the language and resource libraries), its progress bar, and the product version of the executable |
| `menu` | The main menu: what is set when it appears (background of the expansion flags, Continue and its tooltip, version label), what Exit does, the credits pages that replace one another on the interface clock, the scenario and freeform screens (`menu/screens`: lists, pictures, starting cash), and the files that Continue and Play ask the game to open |
| `lists` | The data of those screens: the scenarios of `scenar*.cfg` with results and locks, the freeform maps of `free*.cfg`, the starting cash and its difficulty |
| `world` | The map as the tools change it: the zoo with the "in the zoo" flag of every tile (a fill from beyond the entrance, stopped by exhibit fences), an index of the path, quadrants and edge slots of every tile rebuilt from the entities after each edit, the edit primitives (add and remove an entity, set a tile with the edge states of it and its neighbours written again and, when its shape or elevation changed, the objects on it put at the height of its ground again), the undo list of the last press and a dirty mark that makes the next draw stage the map again. It knows the tank exhibits (`tank_at`: the tiles of a tank are rigid for the terrain tools and come along in a non-contiguous step as a whole) and keeps the tank list the picture reads (`zoo.tanks`) in step with them. A new tank is made in `world/tank.rs` (3.9): its ground levelled and lowered by the `tanks.cfg` sink (recorded for undo) and painted with the floor type of its kind, its walls moved to its side of their lines or built anew where another tank stands (the gate that moved in is replaced), its water values (level 0, filled), and the objects it does not allow found (`refused`) before its name; the water itself is in `world/water.rs` (3.10): the app calls `step_water` once per simulation step not paused and not under a dialog, which moves each level one elevation step towards `H + waterOffset` (filled) or 0, `fill` and `drain` are instant while the game is paused, `set_height` cuts the water at once, and two tanks that merge get the mean height and the flag of the larger volume; the pending gate over a tank wall takes the tank height (`wall_height`) and is drawn as its stack with its own platform; a part cut off a tank takes the water of the tank, and an exhibit whose walls no longer fit its kind is replaced by one of the other kind (the morph). The camera picks a tile and world point from a window point. The undo list holds the actions of the last press and release (the first action after `begin` empties it): a placed entity (with the price paid), a removed one (its place in the list and the refund it gave), a piece that took the place of another (`swap`; a path laid on a path of the same type records nothing), tiles with their old state (a terrain stroke, a path's water painted dirt with no price, an accepted session, which holds the session's snapshot and cost), and the name and gate of an exhibit that a fence cut, or that a removed piece separated from another region, even when the line stays closed, or that a piece turned into a tank or a land exhibit of the other kind (a morph) (recorded before the piece, so the undo restores them after it); a wall brought to the tank's side is a `Moved` action, a wall the wall step copied a `Copied` one (its undo gives back the full price of its type), the removal of one of a tank's own walls first records the tank's purity, salinity, filled flag, wall height and base level (`Tank` actions, no price) and the levelling and floor of a new tank one tiles action without a price. `decline_tank` is a call of `undo`, and the game books the prices of the steps it returns. `priced` tells the price of the action recorded last. `undo` takes the actions back, the last first, and returns what it did with the prices for the game to move in the cash; the exhibits are found again quietly (no name asked, no gate chosen, the counter stays) before each recorded exhibit is given its name and gate. A removed entity is tried at the end of the list first and put at its old place only when it finds its quadrants, slot or path free; one that does not stops the undo (the rest of the list is kept) unless the list holds several removed entities, when it is passed over. The gate tool clears the list. |
| `exhibit` | The regions of the map: tiles joined through sides without an exhibit fence form one region; the one with the entrance is the zoo grounds, the tiles beyond the zoo wall the exterior, every other region an exhibit with its number (kept while it exists), name, gate piece and size. The world builds them when a map opens, and after an exhibit fence is placed, removed or replaced it finds the regions again and matches them with the exhibits there were (`world/exhibit.rs`): a part cut off from the grounds is a new exhibit with a default name (`Event::New`) and a gate chosen by the walk round its boundary with the keeper reachability test once the player has named it (`give_gate`; the gate piece of a line that stays closed moves to the piece left on the line), an exhibit cut in two keeps the side of its gate, one opened to the grounds is deleted and two that join leave one (its gate piece turns back into a fence). An exhibit whose every boundary line holds a tank wall is a tank (`Exhibit::tank`; a show tank when its walls are show walls, `World::retank`), decided again after every fence change, for the maps' stored tanks too (`check-exhibits` compares both counts), and its default name says "Tank" or "Show Tank". The game asks about the objects a new tank does not allow before the name (`app/exhibits.rs`: Yes sells the scenery through the bulldozer's removal and puts animals and staff outside, anything else undoes the operation with its money, `World::decline_tank`). Each exhibit keeps its tiles in the original's fill order (`exhibit::fill`, started at the tile beside the placed piece, the survivor's tile of a removed one or, for an exhibit of a loaded map, its tile on the line the file closes last) and `Exhibits::boundary` reads its boundary pairs from that list; the levelling and refused objects of a new tank walk the tile list, its wall step the boundary list, the gate a land exhibit gets when its own moves into a new tank, the tile outside a new tank where units are put and the morph start take the first fitting pair. A morph without a gate starts at the changed line, stepped by the gate walk while outside the zoo, and a loaded exhibit whose record stores no gate gets the gate walk. An exhibit found in a loaded map takes its gate from its record (`load_gates`: the piece in the stored tile's slot of the stored side). `gate_for` says which exhibit a fence piece could be the gate of (one side in an exhibit, the other on the grounds, the slot across empty, not its gate already) and `move_gate` makes the piece the gate, the old one a fence again, and clears the undo list. The world only tells what happened (`take_events`); the game logs it, asks the name in a modal dialog inside the purchase of the piece that closed the exhibit (the rest of the drag waits and is bought after the answer, the gate and its messages) (`app/exhibits.rs`). `exhibit::rating` rates an exhibit for each species in it (habitat rating): `rate` takes a plain description (`Input`: the tile counts, the objects in the slots, the animals) and gives a `Record` per species with the part scores, the shown suitability (`ceil(max(0, rating))`) and the recommendation bits; `State`, kept in `Exhibit::rating`, holds the 7 s rating timer and the 8 s species timer (`update(ms, rand, input)` takes the step and the simulation's draw as parameters, builds the description only when something is due and restarts a timer at `rand() % 200`), the species list with the tile of each ambient sound, the attractiveness, the assigned keeper and the time last serviced; `input` reads an exhibit of a world into the description. `check-rating <map>` prints the rating of every exhibit per species. `exhibit::viewing` builds the viewing areas of an exhibit (habitat rating 10: `Plan` is the exhibit and its path tiles, `areas` groups the path tiles within 2 tiles of a fenced tile into areas of at most 4 x 4 tiles, `Ground` is the heights and blockers of the map and `Area::observe` finds the exhibit tiles an area sees within 10 tiles in line of sight) and the fence deterioration state (`wear`, rule 12: sound, decayed or broken per exhibit); `Area::pressure` is the guest pressure on one animal for the unit code to apply. Nothing is cached: the callers build the areas again when a path or fence changes and call `wear` after a fence change and a load. `check-rating` also prints each exhibit's areas and deterioration. The world manager has not called `update` yet (the step driver does, once per exhibit and step). |
| `money` | The cash (one `f32`) and the accounts: 31 categories, each with a ring of 12 months, a ring of 20 years and a total; charge (refused when the cash does not cover it), spend (never refused), refund and their undo book into a category and the net line; `can_pay` (a cost of 0 or at most the cash); the month change that clears the new month's column (the year's on January); the cash a start sets, never booked; the status bar text and its three colours. Read from the saved game by `Zoo::parse_money` |
| `panel` | The information panels. `panel::exhibit` is the exhibit panel (information panels 3.5, 10) and the keeper recommendations plaque: `Panel` holds the current exhibit, the animal of the plaque and the refresh clocks (`Clock`: 10 s on the interface clock, not while the game is paused, a forced refresh at once and the next call again), `refresh` writes the list of the exhibits (name and the icon of its fence state, or of a tank's water purity: `tank_icon`), the name field, the construction date, the upkeep, keeper, popularity (the 11-picture half-star set) and donations of `exhibit::Stats` and the animal and thought lists into the layout (an exhibit that becomes current shows the selected tab's sub-layout and hides the others), and returns the `panel` log lines of what changed; `refresh_plaque` rates the animal's exhibit from the world and lists its recommendations (`recommendations`: the important bits first, in red, then the others, the lines about disliked scenery (`rating::disliked`) after the bits of their importance, or the well-suited text; the texts name the animal itself). The panel shows no suitability and has no keeper button (13.5); a row of its animal list selects that animal. `panel::animal` is the animal panel (information panels 6, animals 13): `Panel` holds the selected animal and its 5 s clock, `bars` gives the four bar values and the colour of the suitability bar from the habitat check's thresholds, `band` the three "time ago" texts of the calendar time since the animal last ate and slept (`Animal::fed`, `slept`), and `refresh` writes the layout (the bars through `Interface::set_meter`, the tooltips with their value) and returns its `panel animal` log line when something changed. `app/panel.rs` opens the exhibit panel (a release on the map with no tool picks the gate of an exhibit; the exhibit list button), follows its list, name field and tabs, selects an animal under the pointer and opens its panel (the recommendations button shows the plaque, the exhibit button the exhibit panel, the follow button centres the camera on the animal at each interface pass and stops the keys and edge scroll), and calls `refresh` from the pass |
| `status` | The Finances tab of the zoo status window: the table of 13 rows by 4 months (the current one and the three before it, blank before the accounts begin) as texts from the accounts, income cells as given by the layout, TOTALS and the three amounts below coloured by sign; written into the interface when the tab is shown and then at most every 10 s of clock while the window is shown, changed cells only. `status::zoo` is the zoo status of the game manager: the economy file's settings, the sums over the entities (animals, zoo value, esthetics, species), the rating (7 parts) and its check (also the last step of every load, `Sim::loaded`, which restarts the rating timer), the advice message check, the donation when the cash is low and the three bars; guests, research, the escape penalty and the endowment come with their issues. Log lines `rating N`, `message ID`, `donation AMOUNT` |
| `panel` | The information panels. `panel::exhibit` is the exhibit panel (information panels 3.5, 10) and the keeper recommendations plaque: `Panel` holds the current exhibit, the animal of the plaque and the refresh clocks (`Clock`: 10 s on the interface clock, not while the game is paused, a forced refresh at once and the next call again), `refresh` writes the list of the exhibits (name and the icon of its fence state, or of a tank's water purity: `tank_icon`), the name field, the construction date, the upkeep, keeper, popularity (the 11-picture half-star set) and donations of `exhibit::Stats` and the animal and thought lists into the layout, and returns the `panel` log lines of what changed; `refresh_plaque` rates the animal's exhibit from the world and lists its recommendations (`recommendations`: the important bits first, in red, then the others, the lines about disliked scenery (`rating::disliked`) after the bits of their importance, or the well-suited text; the texts name the animal itself). | `status` | The Finances tab of the zoo status window: the table of 13 rows by 4 months (the current one and the three before it, blank before the accounts begin) as texts from the accounts, income cells as given by the layout, TOTALS and the three amounts below coloured by sign; written into the interface when the tab is shown and then at most every 10 s of clock while the window is shown, changed cells only. `status::zoo` is the zoo status of the game manager: the economy file's settings, the sums over the entities (animals, zoo value, esthetics, species), the rating (7 parts) and its check (also the last step of every load, `Sim::loaded`, which restarts the rating timer), the advice message check, the donation when the cash is low and the three bars; the new guest check (the price column and the chance of the rating, one draw), research, the escape penalty and the endowment payments come with their issues; the guests' needs do too, so the sums count guests and their happiness only. Log lines `rating N`, `message ID`, `donation AMOUNT` |
| `sim` | The simulation step: `Sim::step` runs the managers in the original's order (the game manager with the calendar and its month flag acted on one step late and the zoo status checks, the generator seeded again inside the month-end check, after the other checks, the AI manager with the AI clock, the removal pass, the loop over the entities taken by count and the event queue, the world manager with the zoo grounds' and the exhibits' rating timers, the tank water and the world clock), the `[AI]` keys of the ini file, and the game's random generator (the C runtime's formula, seeded from the wall clock, or from `--seed` plus a count in a test run, or from the seconds a log tells in a replay). The event queue a map stores (from version 97) is restored at a new game, and so are its animals (`Sim::load`, with their draws, before the first step). `Sim::step_in` is `step` with the game's catalogue, which the animals' keys and the exhibits' ratings read, and `Sim::step_game` is `step_in` with the cash and accounts the zoo status reads and books into (and moves to the next month). A unit's own turn is `unit::update`, called from the loop. `sim::drive` runs a world without an interface (`Run`: the step, a script's commands, the cash's month change) for the `sim` command, and `sim::hash::state` hashes its state (see Determinism). The sex of the next hire (`Sim::hire_male`) is drawn from the generator at the start of the program, right after its first seeding, and after every hire (bit 16 of the state, unit placing 4.3), and is part of the state hash with the name counters. |
| `unit` | The live state of animals, guests, keepers, maintenance workers and tour guides: `World::units`, a side table keyed by entity id that follows the entity list (a unit added, removed or restored by an undo enters or leaves it; a new one starts its status timer at `id % period`), and the per-unit update of the original (`unit::update`: events in; unless carried, the move; the animation, scaled by the speed percentage while moving; unless carried, the status timer with its remainder and the needs and checks once it passes the period; the wait, else the behaviour and the goals; the events discarded). What the start, move, needs, checks, animation, behaviour and goals do is the `Hooks` trait (`typed` is the first turn of every unit, where it reads the characteristics of its type, and `start` that of a unit that was not loaded); `Standard` runs the animals' start, needs and checks (`animal`), the move (`unit::movement`: the distance of a step, the walk along the path with its corners, the entry of a tile into the map's unit index, the height on the ground, the guest's speed, the move call of a set with the rate, the animation and the animation speed of its speed band, the gate ahead opened and the gate behind closed, and a swimmer's animation picked again at the water; the move animation is the player's base only (the sequence playing goes on; the unit moves once it shows) and the player's cut, set by a forced set start and by the stop of a walk, shows it at the next advance; the walk ends in the step the unit enters its destination tile, an aborted walk included, and keeps its move animation; the original's loss of the remainder and of a long diagonal step are kept), the random walk (`unit::walk`: the destination by class, the ring, the fallbacks and the original's order of draws), fills the animation (the player of the sprite files' frames, advanced by the step) and the behaviour (`behaviour::Runner`, with the data it reads through `behaviour::Data`), and runs the one-in-15 removal of a unit that stands outside the zoo, which flags the unit for the next removal pass and drops it from the undo list. The state hash covers the walk flags, the band, the gate, the ring and the finder's clearing time and unreachable list. |
| `goal` | The goals of the units (goals spec): the record every unit carries (task, mode, target entity, tile and position, job, job target, start in AI ticks, duration, the done flags), the goal and sub-goal objects built lazily from it by the factory at the next goal update (setting a task only marks the object stale; an object whose init fails clears the record), and `goal::update`, one unit's goal update after its behaviour: the kind's ladder (an animal that is dying, crated or an egg has its record cleared at every update and the goal update ends there, with no draw; a guest keeps its task, stands while its wandered flag is up and lowers it with a draw of 1 in 15, else wanders), the common part (one draw per update, the removal of a unit outside the zoo one time in 15), the goal object, then the sub-goal object. The base goal (tasks 0 and 1) makes a unit that keeps moving wander: it asks for a random destination and starts `bWalk` forced through `Cx::start_walk` (the plain start: a running walk starts again, the sequence is kept, it fails when the type has no calls for it); an animal's wander only sets its destination and target and starts no set. It is done on the target tile when the top set has fewer than two calls left. Task 0x65 of a guest is the first other goal (arrive, then the gawk task); the per-kind goals of the later issues are variants of `Goal` and rungs of the ladder, and the chase task stays off in M6. The random destination is the neighbour fallback of the movement spec until the random walk of issue 338 replaces it. Log family `unit`: `goal ID TASK MODE TARGET TILE` when the task changes and `job ID JOB` when the job does, with the original's names |
| `animal` | The status variables of an animal (happiness, hunger, energy, health, the dung meter, each with a pending change applied at the status tick and clamped to the species' limits), the counters drawn at creation (16 draws, 17 for a young animal; an animal of a loaded map gets them at the load and then the values and counters its record stores, `zoo::AnimalRecord`, so its first turn draws nothing), and the conditional checks that need no other entity, as plain functions of the animal, an environment read from the world and the check periods of `animals.ai`: the `AnimalResetInterval` countdown, keeper visit, mourning, the escape check's rampage (its draws, flag and counter, kept across a load), captivity (below the threshold two draws, and the ground under the animal may become trampled: the hook paints it and rates the exhibit again), social (with the original's flaw for a show, issue 317; the adults and the room count the exhibit's amphibious neighbours, `Exhibit::amphibious`, which nothing links yet), habitat (from the exhibit's rating, which `Sim::step_in` keeps up to date in the world manager, and its other-animals sum `Record::other`), bored (the thirst flag), other animals, the zap draw (the escape, rampage and fence-test effects are M7's) and the "not happy" and "dislikes its exhibit" messages; the end of the checks sets `Animal::ambient`, the request for the behaviour runner's ambient choice. Log family `animal`: `status`, `message`, `trample` |
| `guest` | The guests: the new guest check of the zoo status lets one in at the zoo entrance tile (`arrive`: no guest while the last sums are at `[AI] maxGuests`, the type with equal chance, the status values, check countdowns, sex, age, favourite species and speed offset drawn in the original's order, the name "Guest N" from one counter, the admission of `price x cAdmissionMultiple`, half for a child, booked as admissions income with the counts of admissions and guests created); `Guest` is the live state in `Part::Guest`. Log line `guest add ID adult\|child`. Missing so far: the needs and checks that use the values and countdowns, the way out (the leave goal), the Halloween colours, entrance tiles other than the map's zoo entrance |

| `build` | The construction and objects panels: which panel is shown (noticed from the layouts, so the left bar, the close button, Escape and the right button all work), the item list of each tab (the types of the tab's purchase tag that are available, sorted by the tab's rule: cost, or the fence and habitat orders), the item and the rows each tab remembers, the name, cost, era, fence descriptor and location and habitat pictures of the chosen item (from `ui/infoimg.cfg`, hidden when the habitat is 9410 or 9414), the rotate buttons and the rotation step of each panel. The item chosen is the tool. The bulldozer button (1028, a toggle noticed from its state) is the bulldozer tool and clears the item and the terrain tab; Escape and the right button leave it first; a press on the map keeps its tile and window point, and it is an area drag once the pointer leaves the tile or goes more than 16 pixels from the press point on an axis, and a path as the tool starts a drag (the first tile and the mode of its first leg). | `path` | The path finder: `Finder::request` answers a unit's request for a path to a tile (the cases that need no search, the unreachable list with its counters and its clearing by AI tick, which each simulation step gives as `Finder::tick(now)`), `search` is the A* over tiles with the original's node cap, estimate and open list (`path/search.rs`: the same heap algorithms, so equal costs break ties as the original does), and `path/cost.rs` holds the costs of a step for a `Mover` (keeper, worker, tour guide, guest or animal with the values of its type): the edge cost (fence and cliff tests by kind, the corner of a path), the terrain cost, and whether objects let it through by quarter. `Settings::from_ini` reads the `[AI]` keys. | `place` | The pending object: the chosen type snapped under the mouse (quarter tile, tile centre, tile side or per-axis border by footprint), lifted to the ground, turned by the panel's step and the view, and checked (in the zoo, free quadrants, a path or fence replacing a piece) into a verdict with the first message and the objects in the way. The scenery checks (land or water by `cLand`, `cSwims` and `cOnlySwims`, flat ground at one height for more than a quarter tile, no fence inside a footprint over a tile) are here too, and `place` adds a valid pending object to the world and charges its price as construction, or answers with the message to show (a water-only type on land gets its water message only at the release). The core draws in software into an RGBA framebuffer (`Framebuffer`), as the original's software blitter does. The window only presents that framebuffer. Its pixels are the original's 16 bit screen: every colour drawn is reduced to 5-6-5 bits at once (`sprite::screen`) and kept as the 8 bit value the display shows (high bits repeated), and blends and shadows work on the codes behind it. There is no tint and no time-of-day setting. Every result can therefore be checked without a screen: text output, PNG pictures, hashes.

## Platform

SDL3 (window, input, audio) is built from source and linked statically, so no system SDL is needed. `run` plays a map in the window with the loop of the original; the keys of the map view become commands in `src/input.rs`, and the loop reads the cursor for the edge scroll. A run starts in full screen when `[user] fullscreen` of the ini file is not 0 (windowed run only), and every switch that succeeds is written back to the settings of the user folder. Full screen is switched by Alt+Enter or a script; the window is asked, and when it accepts (or reports on its own that it has switched, which is read from the window flags on every pass) the loop tells the game with a `fullscreen` command, so a log replays it. A full-screen window without the focus blocks the loop like a minimised one. The camera owns the two full-screen rules (edge band, zoom limit); `src/window/fullscreen.rs` places the picture on the display. A looping voice (the menu music) is refilled by SDL's get callback of its stream, on the audio thread, so it keeps playing, muted with the focus, while the loop blocks. `window-smoke` presents a test pattern.

### The window

`rustyzoo run <map>` (`src/window/`) plays the map in a window; `run` without a map starts up (the intro movies, then `src/boot.rs`: picture and progress bar, game data, interface) and opens the main menu, an `App` without a game that acts on what the menu's buttons select (a button that starts a game loads the file inside the click, hides the menu and starts the music's fade; Escape, the options panel's Main Menu and the window close ask first and No goes back to the menu, which `run <map>`, having none, answers by ending); `run --headless` (`src/run.rs`) runs the same `App::pass` on a scripted clock without any window, and prints the log lines that `run --log` also writes, so a log replayed headless gives the same frame hashes.

- **Loop.** As in the original: take every waiting event, run one `App::pass` (draw, interface, simulation timers against one clock reading), present a frame only when the pass painted one or the system asks for a redraw, then wait for an event or until the next timer is due. No spinning. While the window is hidden or minimised the loop blocks. That state is read from the window flags on every iteration, not tracked through the Minimized and Restored events, so it cannot stay asleep after a restore that comes as another event.
- **Sizes.** The frame has the game size (the size of the ini file, cut to the usable area of the display, or `--size`), in a streaming texture that SDL scales to the output with the nearest pixel. The window is enlarged by a whole factor `k`: the display scale (the content scale of the display times its pixel density, read before the window exists) rounded, at least 1, and lowered until the window fits in the usable area. The window is asked for in points, `game * k / density`, with a high pixel density: on Windows and X11 (density 1, content scale 2 at 200%) that is `game * k` points, on macOS and Wayland (density 2, content scale 1) it is the game size in points. So the output has `game * k` pixels on every system, as large and as sharp as the original's window at that scaling. The mouse position is converted from points of the window to pixels of the game. The run checks that the renderer's output equals the window in pixels (a different size than `game * k` only warns, as with a fractional density), and prints the game size and `k`, the window size, its size in pixels, the output size, the display scale and the pixel density. `k` is fixed when the window opens, and a change of the display scale (another display, another setting) is not followed. The window can be resized: the game keeps its size, and the picture is scaled by the largest whole factor that fits and centred on black, the same placing as in full screen (`src/window/fullscreen.rs`), with the mouse mapped back the same way. The maximise button goes to full screen (the window is restored first, so leaving full screen gives a normal window); the zoom limit of a full screen is applied by that switch, and a plain resize does not change the game size, so it has nothing to check.
- **Measures.** `--timing MS` prints the gaps between the firings of each timer on the real clock (count, minimum, median, 95th percentile, maximum, the expected period, a histogram). `--shot FILE` reads back the presented pixels into a PNG. Both are information for a person or CI; no value fails a run.
- **CI.** Every system runs the window with the dummy video driver, the software renderer and the dummy audio driver, and checks sizes, painted frames and the replay of the log. One extra step per system opens a window on the runner's real video driver (X11 on a virtual display on Linux, Cocoa, Windows), reads the pixels back with `--shot` and logs the sizes, the scale, the number of colours and the hash (failing only if the window mode breaks or the picture is blank). A separate step on the dummy driver prints the timing summary of a ten second run. Pictures of game data are checked in CI, never published or committed.

## Game data

The game folder is the install folder, the one that holds `zoo.ini`. It is found with `--data <dir>`, then the `ZT_DATA_DIR` environment variable. **Planned:** a configuration file as a third source.

Files are looked up as the game does: in the directories listed in `[resource] path` of `zoo.ini` (the install folder itself if there is no such key), the earlier directory winning, ignoring letter case, with `/` or `\` as separator. Inside a directory the loose files come first, then the archives (`.ztd` and `.zip`); among the archives of one directory the one whose lower-cased name sorts last wins. Texts come from the resource DLLs, not from the archives. Nothing is ever written into the game folder: settings that RustyZoo changes go in the `zoo.ini` of the per-user folder (`userdir`), which holds only those keys and is put on top of the game's `zoo.ini` when the game is opened (`Vfs::overlay_ini`, keys compared ignoring letter case); see the README for the folders.

Everything read from a file is untrusted: malformed data gives an error, never a panic.

## Design choices

Not implemented yet unless said otherwise.

- **Entities.** No class hierarchy: one `Kind` enum (animal, guest, staff, scenery, building, ...) and flat properties read as the original reads them. Code branches on `Kind` only.
- **Behaviour scripts.** The scripts of the data are parsed at load time into an enum of operations and run by a state machine per unit. `behaviour` parses them when a definition file loads (the catalogue keeps them per file). The 53 operations of the program and the three misspelt names of the shipped data (`fFollow`, `fPlayWith`, `fPlayWithSoud`) parse; the three names and every call with defective arguments become a call that fails when run, as in the original, with a warning for the arguments. Any other unknown name is a load error that names the file, the section and the line. `behaviour::Runner` holds, per unit, the chain of sets being run (a nested set runs in place of its parent's current call, and a failure ends the whole chain), and `behaviour::Animation` the sequence being played, the queue and the base animation; each step the unit advances the animation, then executes the set, a done call letting the next start in the same step. The runner reads the sets, the ambient rows, the frames and delays of the animations and the sounds through the `Data` trait; `Library` implements it on the catalogue and the files and reads the frames of an animation when a unit first plays it. The map draws each unit with the animation and frame it holds (`Extra::shown`).
- **Simulation.** Like the original: a variable step (elapsed milliseconds times the speed factor, capped by `MaxSimTick`) and three independent timers (drawing, interface, simulation). A test mode with a fixed seed and fixed steps gives the same state hash on the three systems (`sim`, see Determinism).
- **Text** (from M4). Liberation Sans, which has the metrics of Arial, rendered with `fontdue` (no hinting, no SIMD, so the glyphs are the same on every system). The two font files are embedded unmodified with their licence in `fonts/`. A font is asked for by a face string and a size string as the layout files give them and cached per (face, size); glyphs are cached per font and character. Text is laid out and drawn into a canvas (`Framebuffer`) that the caller has filled with the background colour: smoothed edges blend towards the pixel underneath, so they blend towards the element's own background, as in the original. `text::format` writes the game's templates (`%s %d %m %r`, positional forms), integers, money and dates with fixed US English conventions, from the strings of the string table.
- **Beyond the original.** Improvements such as interface scaling come after parity.

## Determinism

`rustyzoo sim <map> --ticks N` steps a map without an interface, window or frame and prints one hash of the state (`sim::hash::state`: the tiles, entities, units, exhibits, money, zoo status, calendar, clocks, event queue and generator). The hash is FNV-1a over fixed-width fields, little-endian: whole numbers at their own width, a decimal value by the bits of its `f32`, a string or a list after its length as a `u32`. CI runs it on every map of both trials on Linux, Windows and macOS, with steps of 67 ms and of 1000 ms (the long run crosses two month changes), and the job `simhash` fails if the three differ or a map does not run. A new part of the simulation state goes into the hash in the same change, and a test shows that changing it changes the hash. The rules that keep it equal:

- Whole numbers of the original's widths (`i32`, `u32`, `u16`, `u8`); wrapping arithmetic where the original wraps, never an overflow that panics in debug and wraps in release.
- `f32` for money and the other decimal values, as the original, with the operations written in the original's order; no `f64` where the original has a single, no fused multiply-add.
- No transcendental float function (`sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `exp`, `ln`, `powf` and the like) in the simulation: their results differ between libraries. Square root, add, subtract, multiply, divide and comparisons are exact.
- No iteration over a `HashMap` or `HashSet` (the order differs at every run): a map is only looked up, and anything walked is a `Vec` in a fixed order (`ci/check-hash-iteration.sh` fails on a walk over a hash map in the simulation modules it lists; a walk whose order does not matter carries `// order-free: <why>`).
- The generator is the only source of chance (`sim::Rng`), seeded from the seed of the run, never from the clock or the address of anything (in play the clock's seconds are logged, and a replay seeds with them); the time is the sum of the steps.
- Nothing in the step reads the wall clock, the environment, a file or the draw.

## Dependencies

A dependency is added by the milestone that first uses it. Today: `sdl3`, `zip`, `png`, `flate2` and `fontdue`, and a trimmed copy of NihAV's Indeo 5 decoder in `vendor/nihav` (AGPL version 3 or later, pure Rust, no other dependency; see `vendor/README.md`), used by `movie`. It is vendored because NihAV is not published on crates.io; the decoder is third-party code on untrusted data, so a panic inside it is caught and reported as a decode error.

## Tests

- Unit tests build synthetic bytes in code.
- Tests on real data are `#[ignore]` and read `ZT_DATA_DIR`; run them with `cargo test -- --ignored`.
- `README.md` and this document merge as a union (`.gitattributes`), so two changes to the same row do not conflict; `ci/check-doc-rows.sh` fails on a table row with the same first cell as another in its table, or two bullets with the same bold lead in a section, which is what a union merge leaves behind.
- CI builds on Linux, Windows and macOS, runs `window-smoke`, and runs the commands on the two official trials (`ci/check-trials.sh`), including a windowed run on the dummy video driver, a timing summary and a window screenshot on the real video driver (see The window).
- Nothing derived from the game is committed.
